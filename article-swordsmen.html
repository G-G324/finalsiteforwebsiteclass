Top 5 Swordsman Bosses

Bosses · Rankings · Action games

← Back to Features

Bosses in video games are almost a given. It doesn’t matter how far you get into the story or how many hours you have. Their job as a boss is to leave you stumped and teach you a lesson about having fun! But today, I wanted to talk about those who specifically come at you, wielding a sword.

These are all just personal opinions so if there is anyone who you feel deserves a spot or is missing from the list, feel free to comment who.

Number 5 — Augus | Asura’s Wrath

First on the list, we have Augus from Asura’s Wrath. Asura’s former mentor does start the fight with his bare fists but since he picks up a sword in the second phase, it technically counts!

Asura’s Wrath is a niche, over-the-top action anime game that dares to ask “What if Kratos… but anime?” A character who would be really fun at parties, he doesn't care for good or evil. He just likes to punch things sometimes. Which is great! Because Asura also likes to punch things! So they figured why not punch each other and see which one dies first. No, really. They are very simple people. But before this, you get an entire level based around hanging out with your former mentor at a hot springs.

The fight starts on the moon with you staring down your former mentor, and if you choose to interrupt his in-cutscene monologue, he just decks you into a pile of rubble for being too impatient. After that, it’s a cinematic marvel of Asura screaming at Augus while he’s just reveling in every swing. When he realizes that you’re actually worthy of trying against, he whips out his sword! And it is long. That’s right, he has an extending sword that he uses to level the moon and riddle the arena with these slashes that travel through the map as he rushes from one side of the moon to the other. However, what brings him so low on this list is the fact that his boss, spectacular as it is, is more spectacle than challenge since the game he comes from is relatively simple.

Number 4 — The Edge | Furi

The Edge hails from the indie game, Furi, a boss rush where you, the player, must push through a gauntlet of bosses or “jailors” to make your way out of prison. What kind of prison, you might ask? Why, an incredibly large space prison parked stories above each just outside of Earth’s stratosphere. You know, for good measure. As such, the jailors are all hand selected to beat you so bad you go back to your cell out for safety, with some taking a disturbing amount of pleasure doing so…

But then there’s The Edge, who, in this arsenal of badass warriors eager to teach you a lesson, is surprisingly the most chill antagonist you will ever see in a 1v1 boss battle. Whereas most bosses in games are either silently judging you with their non-words and frustrating AI, or constantly mocking you, Edge just wants a good battle.

He plays differently from other bosses in that, for 1, your gun is gone, meaning you can’t pull off cheeky chip damage between swings, 2, you cannot hit him with any charge attack as he’ll just trigger a QTE where if successful, you don't get hit, and should you fail, you take ALL the damage. This also extends to when you land a perfect parry and attack when he’s down. And 3, this guy hits hard!

He has trained for years for the moment to meet the player and fight you, and he doesn’t succumb easily to certain cheap tactics. Also if you even think about hitting him while he’s taunting, he will dodge it every time. You will need to be able to block his attacks and move out of the way when needed. And if you’re anything like me and hate defense, you’ll find it both harder and much scarier trying to interrupt him while he’s in the middle of an attack.

And during his second phase, the man decides that you’re adjusting too fast to his swordplay and finds a paddle to beat you with instead. But throughout the whole fight, in each of his phases, he’ll try to encourage you to get back up and try harder. The only time he doesn’t is during one of his cycled lines where he tells you that you’re not challenging enough and he can’t improve his technique this way. And even then it’s not said harshly. The Edge is just a chill but powerful dude.

Number 3 — Lingering Will | Kingdom Hearts 2: Final Mix

Coming up at the next spot is the Lingering Will from Kingdom Hearts 2: Final Mix. Lingering Will is just that. The lingering will of a character who had gotten possessed in the prequel games. But more than that, one of the protagonists of the prequel, and when you finally beat Kingdom Hearts 2, and are told to check out this weird wormhole in the King’s castle, you’re transported to the same spot the prequel ended to see this guy.

“But wait,” I hear you say, “why would you fight him? You just said he was the protagonist.” Well, my imaginary puppeteered audience member, in the prequel there was a man who looks weirdly like our main protagonist, Sora, and poor old Will mistakes him for… his boss? Wait, why wouldn’t Will confuse him for the guy you look like?

Well, whatever. The point is, he thinks you’re the villain who took his body and possibly killed his friends. Understandably, he’s pissed! Every combo he hits you with is capable of bringing your maxed out HP bar down to 0 in an instant. He cycles through a list of attacks at seemingly random but if you play enough, you can sort of predict what he will do. Somehow. He could hit you with a whirlwind whip attack, fire a homing ball that will stay for the whole match unless you or Goofy can knock it back or, just for the fun of it, run you over by turning his weapon into a motorbike.

If that isn’t enough, he even has the ability to fire an arrow that, if it hits, will lock most of your commands. Including the one you use to attack. And yes, “most,” as there will be one command that will free you but keeps moving around the deck! Meaning, if you try to catch it too early or late and push the wrong one, you die. All you can hope to do is block and evade until you can find the right one as Will continues to pelt you with attacks. And that’s not even all, as midway into the fight, he incorporates little laser-shooting drones to whittle your damage down unless you block them in order of which one charges up first. And if you know KH2, you’ll know that bosses have a move specially for when the AI has had enough of your 50 hit combo. Will is no different. For a spirit, he is oppressive in his moveset!

And if THAT was still too easy for you to handle, he has one more desperation move which he will spam in frustration when he’s near his final health bar. You have to try everything you’ve learned in the game thus far to snap the spirit out of it and make him realize you’re the same 60-year-old man who possibly killed his friends.

But hey, when you do manage to beat him and finally calm him down, you get a nice little crown for your troubles and see the only character acknowledge Sora’s power. It’s no wonder he remains one of the hardest bosses in the series. Yes, behind Yozora.

Number 2 — Vergil | Devil May Cry 3

That’s right! It’s the storm approaching! Specifically, in his Devil May Cry 3 incarnation. If there is anything we here at Passing Time Studios love, it's a good rival, and in every aspect, Vergil takes the cake.

While technically being mentioned and hyped up in the first game, and somewhat seen in the manga and novel, the twin brother of Capcom’s demon slayer made a massive impact on players and the series in his debut. All he wants is power, and to do that, Vergil summons a massive tower that happens to bring creatures of Hell. Meaning, poor Dante has to scale the thing to stop him.

When you first meet him, he gives you just a taste of his skill, already slicing the bullets you shoot at him before lining them up to play an aggressive game of golf. His missed attacks even slice through the environment. Something you can’t do, so it’s best to avoid or parry if you don’t want to end up like those poor pillars. Speaking of parrying, he can actually parry your attack if your combo is getting too long. Now, you can delay your swing to make him whiff it, but the moment he pulls it off, he’s taking his turn back.

Then you face him again midway through the game, where he wants to test out his shiny new gauntlets, and flex some more of his power. Like these summoned swords that can fire at you or follow you around.

But the climax to all this is what we’re really here for. The final level of DMC3 is Vergil in a no-holds-barred battle where he gains new abilities to use against you, and actually use every weapon he has at the moment. Including the Force Edge, which he will use to bully you from afar. But if he doesn’t want to use his father’s blade, there’s always his signature judgment cut, which he will do more times consecutively in hopes of one of them catching you. Which is nothing compared to his arguably cooler prototype of the final judgment cut, where he disappears before filling the screen with slashes.

The reason why I put his 3 boss fight here instead of his battle in DMC5 is simply because it’s nearly unanimously more difficult to face in 3 and he has much more of a presence.

Number 1 — Jetstream Sam | Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance

There will be blood! Jetstream Sam is next on the list and remains one of the more hype battles in the Metal Gear series. Another contender for bosses you’d probably want to hang out with, Sam isn’t exactly evil. He is just really good at his job as a mercenary and hates the system!

Much like Vergil, Sam is another rival to the main character who will pop up throughout the levels. The first time you meet him is in the prologue, where no matter how hard you try to beat him, it will end in him being victorious, taking your left eye and arm as souvenirs.

But on your final encounter, it's payback time! It's just you and Sam in one last, honorable sword fight in a massive arena. He will throw chunks of the environment, soar into the sky for a dive attack, and slice through you, and it's your job to simply keep up, using everything you’ve learned and acquired to turn the tides. It isn’t the most flashy battle in the game. If anything this is the most grounded boss here. But in a game with cyborg ninja riding missiles and punching senators, Sam shows us that you don’t need over-the-top action to make a good, memorable boss fight.
